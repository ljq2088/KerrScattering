## Technical conclusions retained by the agent

1. A Teukolsky radial solution is not identified by its raw complex values
   alone. The radial variable, factored horizon phase, angular eigenvalue, and
   asymptotic normalization must be converted before comparing codes.
2. MST is especially valuable at low frequency because it supplies analytic
   connection and Wronskian information. GSN is especially valuable as a
   numerically stable transformed-equation benchmark, particularly for high
   frequency. They are complementary audits.
3. A flux-balance identity tests global dissipative bookkeeping. It does not
   prove that a local field, a conservative self-force, or an accumulated EMRI
   phase is equally accurate.
4. Gravitational self-force is an expansion in the small body's mass ratio and
   requires a singular/regular field split, gauge control, and worldline
   dynamics. The current scalar calculation is a fixed-background prescribed
   nonlinear field response and must remain labelled that way.
5. Second-order gravitational Teukolsky work is the correct bridge toward a
   genuinely gravitational nonlinear calculation, but it introduces metric,
   tetrad, reconstruction, gauge, and infrared structures absent from the
   scalar toy model.
6. A frequency-domain Lorentzian fit to a real-frequency response is not a QNM
   excitation coefficient. Kerr QNM projection requires the appropriate
   conserved bilinear form and regularization/analytic-continuation treatment.
7. EMRI readiness is ultimately a long-time requirement: source-mode accuracy
   must be propagated through orbital evolution and phase accumulation. The
   current project establishes a radial-response module, not a waveform model.
8. The reusable dependency graph is now explicit: geodesic frequencies feed a
   discrete source-harmonic lattice, the radial solver feeds mode amplitudes,
   and only after regularization and orbit evolution can one discuss a genuine
self-force waveform observable.

9. Recent second-order work reinforces the boundary of the present manuscript:
   a cubic fixed-background scalar source is a controlled nonlinear response
   problem, while a genuine second-order gravitational self-force calculation
   needs a puncture/regular field, gauge and reconstruction data, and slow-time
   orbital evolution.

10. The latest numerical frontier separates three error axes: perturbative
    order, field discretization, and source/model truncation. A high-order
    radial solve is necessary for an EMRI backend but cannot by itself certify
    a long-time waveform phase.

11. The EMRI waveform layer is best learned through a concrete transfer chain:
    horizon/infinity amplitudes become fluxes, fluxes drive adiabatic orbital
    evolution, and the evolving orbital frequencies generate multiple phase
    voices. The single-frequency radial response is therefore a backend, not
    the final observable.
