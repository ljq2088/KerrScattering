# Cover Letter Template

Dear Editors of Physical Review D,

We submit the manuscript

> Nonuniform weak-coupling expansion in scalar scattering from near-extremal Kerr

for consideration as a Regular Article in Physical Review D.

The manuscript formulates and computes a weakly nonlinear scattering problem
for a self-interacting complex scalar field on Kerr spacetime.  It extends the
flux-based nonlinear Green-function construction used in the Schwarzschild
case to the scalar Teukolsky equation with spin weight `s=0`.  The Kerr
extension keeps the physical definition of transmission and reflection
coefficients fixed while incorporating the co-rotating horizon frequency,
scalar spheroidal harmonics, and the Sigma-weighted nonlinear source.  The
`U(1)`-invariant interaction closes at the incident frequency, and the reported
first-order flux coefficients are defined per unit incident-amplitude squared.

The calculation first establishes its normalization and numerical controls in
the axisymmetric sector.  Matched low-frequency analysis gives a finite,
spin-independent cubic response and the exact monopole value
`chi_H(0)=log(2)/pi`; for the general interaction `|Phi|^(2n) Phi`, the same
monopole instead scales as `(M omega)^(2n-2)`.  The moderate-frequency spectra
contain resonance-like features and finite-window exponential tails, but the
manuscript explicitly separates those observations from quasinormal-mode
residues or an exactly thermal law.

The main result is a nonuniform weak-coupling expansion in principal
near-superradiant modes.  Paired-resolution calculations through
`a/M=0.9999984375` support
`M^(2n-2) chi_H^(n)=(M kappa)^(-n) F_(n,l)` for `n=1,2,3`, with bounded
finite-window modulation.  Real-conformal-weight control modes do not obey
this hierarchy.  A new finite-bandwidth audit shows that integration over a
fixed `p/kappa` interval removes only one inverse power, so the `n=2,3`
windowed weights retain `kappa^(-1)` and `kappa^(-2)` enhancement, while the
direct cubic control remains finite at leading power.  Dense
profiles at `a/M=0.9999` further show that increasing the operator order moves
the response peak toward the absorptive side and narrows it within the common
`p=O(kappa)` scale.  These results show that the fixed-order weak-coupling
expansion is nonuniform as the extremal limit is approached.  For a potential
containing several local powers, the derived adjacent-order ratio also shows
how the coefficient-defined operator hierarchy can invert before the
fixed-order expansion loses control.

Numerical accuracy is assessed by spectral tails, field-dependent off-grid
residuals, paired resolutions, Wronskian constancy, and both linear and
first-order flux balance.  Acceptance is path dependent: a row must pass the
paired-response, linear-balance, and Wronskian gates, together with either an
absolute or a flux-relative first-order balance gate when cancellation makes
the absolute residual uninformative.

The work should be of interest to readers studying black-hole scattering,
Kerr perturbation theory, nonlinear response functions, and frequency-domain
methods for black-hole physics.

referees upon request.  We will deposit a versioned public archival release
with a persistent identifier before publication.

This manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under
